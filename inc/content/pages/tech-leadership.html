Engineering Leadership & Architecture

The step from senior to staff is not more code — it is scope. This track is a set of practices: how to write a decision that survives you, how to disagree productively, how to run an incident, and how to grow the engineers around you.

description

A decision document that holds

A written RFC with options, trade-offs, rollout plan and measurable success criteria, reviewed asynchronously by engineers who did not attend the meeting.

CURRICULUM

Six modules, in order

Each module ends with reviewed work. Later modules assume the earlier ones are done — that is the point.

Scope, influence without authority, the difference between senior and staff work, and how to find the problems worth solving.
RFC and ADR structure, how to present options honestly, how to socialise a proposal before the review, and how to record the outcome.
Trade-off analysis, reversibility, migration strategy and the discipline of saying no to a technically interesting idea.
Incident command, communication during failure, blameless review, and turning action items into shipped changes.
Feedback that lands, delegation, mentoring relationships and performance conversations you do not dread.
Estimating, sequencing, negotiating scope with product and finance, and reporting progress without theatre.
Module list is the published curriculum for the current cohort. Ordering and emphasis are adjusted between cohorts as tooling and hiring practice change; the learning outcomes do not.
